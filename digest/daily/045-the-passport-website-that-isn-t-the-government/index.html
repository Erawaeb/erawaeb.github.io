The passport website that isn't the government — October 29, 2026.

THE BE AWARE DIGEST DAILY RED FLAG #045 — OCT 29, 2026

THE SCAM

The passport website that isn't the government.

You need to renew your passport, so you search "passport renewal online." The top result is a sponsored ad that looks official: an American flag seal, pictures of passports, a page that lists the "Issuing Agency" as the U.S. Department of State, and a big blue "Get My Passport" button.

You pay, and the confirmation screen says "Your passport application has been submitted." But nothing was submitted to the State Department. Customers mostly got paperwork that is free on the government's own site.

On October 22, the FTC sued the companies behind the operation — GovAssist LLC, Entry LLC, and their owner — saying they collected at least $40 million from more than 300,000 consumers this way since 2023. The sites include GovAssist.com, Entry.com, and IVS.us, whose badge-style logo the FTC says is meant to echo the old Immigration and Naturalization Service.

THE RED FLAG

It shows up above the real thing. For queries like "apply for a new passport," the defendants' paid ads appeared over the State Department's own website. Sponsored results are the first thing everyone clicks.

The names are built to sound official: "Gov" in GovAssist, ".us" in the URL, "Entry" echoing the real Global Entry program. And the confirmation screen does the heavy lifting — it says your application was "submitted" without ever saying submitted to whom. The federal government issues passports at one place online; it does not run eight lookalike domains.

THE RULE

For anything government — passports, taxes, benefits — never start from a sponsored search result. Scroll past the "Sponsored" block, or type the agency's own address yourself. Passport renewals go through travel.state.gov, and the application form itself is free — anyone charging you a big fee just for the paperwork is selling you nothing.

If you already paid one of these sites, check your order status on the State Department's real site, and report the ad at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

(Source: FTC lawsuit filed October 22, 2026 (via Techlicious, BakerHostetler; allegations not yet tested in court))
