THE BE AWARE DIGEST DAILY RED FLAG #015 — SEP 27, 2026

THE SCAM

The 'grandson' who needs bail money.

You get a call, and the voice on the other end says 'Hi Grandma, it's me.' It sounds a little off — they say they have a cold, or the line is bad. Then the story comes out: they were in a car accident, or they've been arrested, and they need money right now. Bail, a lawyer, hospital bills. And one more thing: 'Please don't tell Mom and Dad, they'll be so upset.' The panic is the whole trick. You're not thinking anymore, you're reacting — and the money goes out by wire, gift cards, or a payment app before anyone stops to check.

THE RED FLAG

The caller begs you to keep it secret. That's the giveaway. A real emergency doesn't need secrecy from the rest of the family — a scam does, because one phone call to your real grandchild or their parents ends it in seconds. The other tell is the payment method: wire transfers, gift cards, and payment apps are how scammers get paid, because once the money is sent, it's gone.

THE RULE

Hang up, then call back on a number you already have. Look up your grandchild's real number — or call their parents — and ask whether anything actually happened. Don't hit redial, and don't trust the number on your screen; scammers can fake caller ID. Then pass this one on: family group chats stop this scam cold, because the first person to check in ruins the whole act.

(Source: FTC consumer alerts; long-documented family-emergency ('grandparent') scam pattern)
